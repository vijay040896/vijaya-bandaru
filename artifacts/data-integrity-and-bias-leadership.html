Data Integrity as a Foundation for Ethical AI

Objective

I created this artifact to connect two pieces of work that turned out to be more related than they first appeared: a hands-on data-cleaning project for a concession-sales dataset, and a leadership reflection on navigating human bias in AI. My objective was to show that the discipline of rigorous data cleaning isn't just a technical exercise, it's the same discipline that prevents biased data from becoming biased AI in the first place.

Process

On the technical side, I audited a 214-row raw sales dataset and found five categories of problems: duplicate and colliding transaction IDs, missing values, inconsistent formatting (misspelled items, inconsistent capitalization), outliers and impossible values (a quantity of 500 sold, a $99.99 price on a $3 item), and calculated fields (Revenue, Actual_Profit) that didn't match their own formulas. I fixed each category using evidence already present in the data, cross-referencing fields against each other, rather than guessing, and documented every fix in a change log. I then applied PivotTable-style summaries, a live SUMPRODUCT array formula, and a Solver-style optimization model to the cleaned data.

On the leadership side, I worked through a class discussion on how human bias enters AI systems, specifically through data selection, labeling, algorithm design, testing and evaluation, and interpretation of results, and used that to write a personal value statement and field-specific strategies for navigating bias as a leader in Appian-based enterprise AI development.

Work Sample — Where the Two Connect:

From the bias leadership reflection: "Appian's Document Extraction & Classification AI Skill depends entirely on labeled training data. Whoever labels those documents brings their own judgment calls, so labeling guidelines need periodic review, and ambiguous cases should be labeled by more than one person to catch individual bias before it becomes the model's baseline."

That is precisely the failure mode I was hunting for, row by row, in the concessions dataset: values that looked plausible on their own but didn't hold up once cross-checked (a category that didn't match its item, a margin that didn't match its profit). The technical habit of not trusting a value until it's been checked against other evidence is the same habit that catches bias before it gets baked into a model.

Tools

Python (pandas) and Excel for the data-cleaning and PivotTable/SUMPRODUCT/Solver analysis; Claude (Anthropic) to help draft and refine the bias-leadership value statement and strategies.

Value Proposition

This artifact shows a range I don't think either piece would show alone: the technical rigor to actually find and fix real data-quality problems, and the leadership awareness to explain why that rigor matters ethically, not just technically. For a field increasingly built on AI systems trained on real organizational data, that combination, someone who can both clean the data and lead a team in caring about why it needs to be clean, is exactly the kind of range I want my portfolio to demonstrate.
